The idea of a single tree slowly walking across the landscape by dropping aerial roots that turn into new trunks captures exactly how actual Ficus benghalensis operates in real life.

Here is a breakdown of how that mechanic could work conceptually, along with a few design considerations to keep performance smooth and integration natural.

🌳 How the Growth Mechanics Could Work
To make this feel organic while staying within NeoForge performance budgets, you can break its growth cycle into clear phases:

[Phase 1: Main Trunk] ──> [Phase 2: Canopy Spread] ──> [Phase 3: Aerial Root Drop] ──> [Phase 4: Root Anchor] ──> [Phase 5: Sub-Trunk & Propagation]
1. Primary Growth & Canopy Extension
Initial Sprout: The sapling grows into a main central trunk (say, 6–10 blocks tall) and begins generating a thick top canopy.

Horizontal Branching: Special Branch Logs (or directional log blocks) extend outward horizontally through random scheduled block ticks.

Distance Detection: Once a branch block reaches a threshold distance (∼4–5 blocks horizontally from the nearest active trunk), it gains the ability to trigger an aerial root drop.

2. Dropping Aerial Roots
Instead of instantly spawning solid wood down to the floor, the branch slowly grows a Prop Root / Hanging Vine block downwards during block ticks.

Reaching Ground: The downward root growth checks for solid soil (dirt, grass_block, coarse_dirt, etc.).

Thickening Transformation: Once the tip makes contact with soil, the vertical root column transforms into full Banyan Trunk Logs, anchoring into the ground and becoming a new "Sub-Trunk."

3. Expansion & Propagating
The newly established Sub-Trunk now acts as a local anchor point, letting the canopy spread further outward and repeating the process to span multiple chunks over time.

🛠️ Design Considerations & Performance Tips
1. Controlling Unchecked Growth (Lag Prevention)
Because a self-propagating tree can theoretically run wild across the whole world, setting boundaries will keep your tick loop happy:

Maximum Radius / Generation Cap: Set a maximum generation limit (e.g., 3–4 generations of sub-trunks, or a max horizontal radius of 24–32 blocks from the original origin trunk).

Soil Requirements: Require specific fertile ground or active hydration for aerial roots to successfully anchor and form full trunks.

Growth Speed: Keep random tick chances low so the expansion happens gradually over many in-game days rather than exploding in minutes.

2. Dense Understory Ecosystem
Since Banyans create dark, dense shade under their vast canopies:

Specialized Foliage: The thick leaf layer provides shade where shade-loving plants, moss, hanging vines, or special fungi could spawn beneath it.

Dynamic Canopy Depth: Higher layers can be leafy and light-permeable, while lower canopy layers stay dense enough to block light and create that twilight understory vibe.

🍃 Player-Driven Cultivation Mechanics
1. Re-Energizing Trunks (Bone Meal Mechanics)
Local Reset: Right-clicking an inactive or capped Banyan Trunk with Bone Meal resets its local growth cooldown or internal generation tier.

Canopy Push: Connected horizontal branches gain a temporary boost to extend further out, letting players stretch a single tree across massive distances or shape custom archways and bridges.

2. Targeted Root Dropping (Sulfur / Catalyst)
Forced Propagation: Applying Sulfur Dust (or a similar modifier) directly to a horizontal branch block forces an immediate Aerial Root Drop straight down from that specific block.

Living Structures: Players can align a row of branches, dust them all, and watch a solid Living Wall, fence, or natural house perimeter grow directly into the dirt.

Custom Density: Solves the issue of random tick RNG—if a player wants a super-dense understory with trunks spaced every 2 blocks instead of 4, they just apply the dust where they want the columns.

In real life, a grove of Quaking Aspens (like the famous Pando clone in Utah) isn't actually a group of individual trees—it's a single massive underground organism connected by a shared root network (rhizome system). Bringing that inverted "underground spread, upward shoots" mechanic into Minecraft is both botanically accurate and mechanically rich.

Here is how the Aspen's underground network could function in-game, along with some great gameplay dynamics it opens up!

🍂 Underground Root System & Shoot Mechanics
[Main Aspen Trunk]
       │
       ▼ (1-2 blocks down)
[Taproot / Central Core] ─── Horizontal Roots (1 block below surface) ───► [New Aspen Shoot]
1. The Underground Network
Taproot Base: Beneath the main trunk, a specialized block (like Aspen Taproot or Rooted Earth) acts as the network hub.

Subsurface Spread: During random ticks, root blocks check for adjacent dirt, coarse dirt, or podzol 1 block under the surface. They spread horizontally up to a set radius, converting normal dirt blocks into Rooted Dirt / Aspen Roots.

2. Sprouting New Shoots
Shoots Above Ground: Once a root block is established under a clear surface block (air, grass, or light vegetation), it has a random chance to sprout an Aspen Sapling / Shoot straight up.

Rapid Growth: Because these shoots draw energy from the mature parent root system, they could grow into full adult Aspen trees significantly faster than a standard planted sapling!

🎮 Player Interaction & Cultivation
Just like with your Banyan idea, giving players tools to direct or manipulate the root network creates awesome gameplay opportunities:

1. Root Pruning & Border Control
Physical Barriers: Placing stone, bricks, or cobblestone down to 2 blocks deep stops the roots from expanding past a boundary—allowing players to keep an Aspen grove confined to a specific yard or garden bed.

Coarse Dirt / Shovel Tilling: Tilling a root block with a hoe or replacing the soil breaks the network connection, isolating a section of the grove.

2. Bone Meal & Fertilizer Synergies
Root Force: Applying Bone Meal to any individual tree in the network can send growth energy through the underground root blocks, causing multiple new shoots to pop up across the entire grove at once!

Shared Health / Defense: Since the grove shares a single root system, fertilizing or caring for one tree nourishes the whole clone colony.

1. Moss Block Subsurface Probing
Scheduled Ticks: Surface Moss Blocks occasionally check the blocks directly beneath them (1–8 blocks deep).

Permeable Media: Roots push through soft materials like dirt, coarse_dirt, rooted_dirt, mud, or clay (stopping at solid stone, deepslate, or obsidian).

Reaching Air: Once the downward search hits an open air block with a surface below, it transforms the ceiling block into a hanging Cave Vine (with a chance for glow berries).

2. Understory & Cave Spawning
When the hanging vine establishes itself over a solid cave floor:

Initial Sprout: It spawns a small surface feature underneath on the cave floor—either an Azalea Bush, a Small Mushroom (red/brown), or a shade-tolerant Evergreen Sapling.

Overgrowth / Bone Meal Cultivation: Left alone, it remains a charming miniature cave plant. But if a player (or automated mechanic) repeatedly Bone Meals the sprout, it forces it to mature into a full underground tree or giant mushroom feature, creating lush underground oases!
